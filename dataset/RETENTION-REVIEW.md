# Capability retention review — 2026-09-20

This is a targeted literature review and proposed experiment, not a claim that
OpenJev has demonstrated capability retention. Most cited experiments concern
autoregressive models; their numerical results do not transfer directly to
DiffusionGemma.

## Evidence

- [Dong et al., ACL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.12/): data amount,
  capability coverage, and training order matter; no universal mixture ratio.
- [Scaling Laws for Forgetting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05605): parameter-efficient
  adaptation still forgets; update count and adaptation capacity matter.
- [Self-Distillation Fine-Tuning, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13669): responses
  generated by the original model can reduce distribution shift during adaptation.
- [Thinking Machines, On-Policy Distillation](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/):
  in a Qwen3-8B internal-document experiment, 30% background chat helped but did
  not fully preserve instruction following. Subsequent distillation from the
  original model recovered most of the loss. This is an experiment, not a 30% rule.
- [On-Policy Replay, May 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29495): filtered
  current-model rollouts improve retention on TRACE at small replay budgets.
  Its budget is defined against historical task data, not our fraction of all
  training examples. Frozen-base cached responses are not the same algorithm.
- [NVIDIA DiffusionGemma SFT recipe](https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo/automodel/recipes-e2e-examples/diffusiongemma):
  uses uniform vocabulary corruption with continuously sampled noise, loss on
  clean and corrupted supervised positions, self-conditioning, and an auxiliary
  encoder autoregressive objective on the shared backbone. These are important
  reference points for our deliberately smaller decoder-only experiment.

## Current implementation audit

The completed pilot trained classification only. Accuracy changes were not
statistically established, game strength did not improve, and canvas-seed
disagreement increased. Temperature-only calibration is an essential control.
No generation, tool-use, reasoning-retention, or image-retention result exists.

The new replay code defaults to 20% example selections. Of the remaining
classification examples, 25% can use cached native thoughts: 60% direct decisions,
20% thought-conditioned decisions, and 20% generation when both inputs exist.
This is a proposal in code, not the recipe used by the completed GPU runs.

Generation replay samples one response block and averages CE on corrupted
positions. It uses four discrete noise levels and no self-conditioning, unlike
the NVIDIA recipe. Encoder and output head remain frozen; adding encoder loss
alone would not train our decoder-only adapters. These deviations need explicit
ablation or alignment before a larger retention claim, not a silent change to
the architecture or a costly eight-GPU launch.

Decision CE is normalized over allowed labels; replay CE is over the full
vocabulary. Both are averaged, so example count, target-token count, and gradient
influence differ. The current 0.1 thought-rehearsal coefficient is unvalidated.
Log component loss, supervised tokens, gradient norms, and sampling frequencies.

The initial NVIDIA build is a bounded file-prefix smoke test, not a representative
replay corpus. Missing upstream prompts are excluded. Template-prefix rejections
must be investigated, especially for tool calls; do not interpret raw agentic
record counts as verified coverage of tool-call targets. Thought/nonthought
variants and all conversation turns must remain in the same split.

## Proposed economical experiment

Start with 20% general replay, not a claim that 20% is sufficient. Keep the
direct/thought-conditioned ratio within decision examples at 3:1 initially.
For 100K total example selections this is 60K direct, 20K thought-conditioned,
20K general replay. For a fixed 100K decision examples, 20% replay of the total
requires 25K general examples (125K total), not 20K.

Compare 0%, 10%, 20%, and 30% replay with matched total optimizer updates first;
report the reduced number of decision exposures at higher replay. Confirm the
best candidates with matched decision exposures and additional seeds if justified.
Do not run the full matrix before serialization and inference checks pass.

Replay must cover ordinary nonthinking responses, reasoning, code/math, tool use,
multi-turn instruction following, and any languages/modalities we claim to retain.
Text-only replay cannot establish vision retention. NVIDIA data supplies useful
coverage; a subset of frozen DiffusionGemma responses can anchor native behavior.
Treat response-generation costs separately from training costs.

Generate classification thoughts without gold labels in the prompt, then verify
the resulting decision against gold or an executable checker. Correct final
answers do not prove faithful reasoning. Reject contradictory or malformed traces;
retain hard examples in direct-decision training to avoid discarding them entirely.
Evaluate fresh adapted-model thoughts, not only cached base-model thoughts.

Use held-out instruction following, executable code/math, structured tool calls,
multi-turn generation, and image tasks alongside decision accuracy, NLL/Brier,
actual reread rate, seed stability, and latency. Keep evaluation sources and near
duplicates out of training. Compare identical inference settings and report paired
uncertainty. A proposed release gate is no more than a 1 percentage-point absolute
drop on each primary capability metric, with a confidence interval narrow enough
to support that margin; a tiny smoke test cannot certify it.

If replay still regresses capabilities, first adjust learning rate, update count,
coverage, and objective fidelity. Then consider frozen-base distribution matching
on replay denoising states. Autoregressive reverse-KL recipes require adaptation
to diffusion; adding their loss verbatim would be unjustified. RL and architectural
isolation are not prerequisites for teaching this decision protocol.
